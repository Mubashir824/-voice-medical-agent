# Polio (poliomyelitis)

## Key facts

- Polio, also known as poliomyelitis, is a contagious disease that can result in paralysis and death.
- Polio is caused by the poliovirus, which can spread through saliva and faeces (poo) from infected people.
- Most people with polio don't have symptoms, but some people experience paralysis that can be permanent or cause death.
- The best way to prevent polio is to get vaccinated.
- Australia has been free of polio since the year 2000, but vaccination is still important to prevent the spread of polio from countries where it still exists.

## What is polio?

Polio is a highly contagious viral infection that can cause paralysis and death. It is also known as poliomyelitis, or infantile paralysis.
Polio was once a widespread disease that affected many people. Now, due to vaccination, polio has been eliminated in most parts of the world.
Australia began routinely vaccinating against polio in 1956. The last polio epidemic in Australia was from 1961 to 1962. Australia was officially declared free of polio in 2000.

## What causes polio?

Polio is caused by the poliovirus.
Poliovirus is very contagious and spreads by contact with infected saliva or faeces (poo). This means polio can spread through contact with:
- contaminated food or water
- droplets produced when an infected person coughs or sneezes
Poliovirus first infects your gastrointestinal tract (gut). In some people, it moves through the bloodstream and also infects the central nervous system .
Unvaccinated people may become infected if they travel to areas with polio. They may also take the infection with them to polio-free countries, including Australia, and could infect others.
For this reason, immunisation is still important to prevent the return of polio to the community.

## What are the symptoms of polio?

Most people with poliovirus have no symptoms, so you might not know that you have the infection. Those who do get symptoms can have poliovirus for up to 35 days before the symptoms appear. This means the virus can infect many people before it is detected.
Some people infected with poliovirus have early symptoms similar to those of gastroenteritis ('gastro') that last for up to 5 days, including:
- diarrhoea
- nausea and vomiting
- fever
- fatigue (tiredness)
Other symptoms of poliovirus include:
- sore throat
- headache
- pain or stiffness in the back or neck
- sensitivity to light
The virus can stay in your faeces for up to 6 weeks once symptoms have appeared.
### Acute flaccid paralysis
Less than 1 in every 100 people who catch poliovirus develops severe muscle weakness. This is also known as acute flaccid paralysis (AFP). This muscle weakness can affect your:
- arms and legs
- head and neck
- diaphragm, which is a muscle needed for breathing
Acute flaccid paralysis can stop you from moving and breathing properly.
Most people recover completely, but for some people, the paralysis is permanent. Severe cases of acute flaccid paralysis, where the breathing muscles become paralysed, can be fatal (cause death).

## When should I see my doctor?

If you have symptoms of acute flaccid paralysis, see a doctor immediately. If you have gastroenteritis-like symptoms and are severely dehydrated , see a doctor.

## How is polio diagnosed?

To diagnose polio, your doctor will first examine you . They will ask if you have:
- been vaccinated against polio
- travelled to a country that still has polio
- been in contact with someone who has travelled to a country with polio
Your doctor will confirm a polio diagnosis by:
- testing a stool (poo) sample
- doing a throat swab
Polio is a notifiable disease . If you are diagnosed with polio, your doctor will tell your local public health unit. They will try to trace the people you have been in contact with. This helps prevent further spread of the disease.

## How is polio treated?

There is no specific treatment for polio. Some treatments can help you recover and prevent complications. These include:
- physiotherapy to aid recovery from paralysis
- intensive care in hospital , including help with breathing
In the past, people with polio who had trouble breathing were placed in special ventilators called 'iron lungs'.

## Can polio be prevented?

Polio can only be prevented by immunisation .
Australia's National Immunisation Program recommends the polio vaccine for:
- children and infants
- adults who are at higher risk of polio
While children under 5 years of age and young adults are most at risk, anyone can get polio.
Adults can be at high risk of catching polio if they:
- travel to countries with polio
- live or work with people with polio
- work in a lab with polio
### What are the recommendations for the polio vaccine?
Vaccination is the best protection against polio.
There are different vaccines available for polio. Talk to your doctor about which one is best for you.
Talk with your doctor if you didn't have the polio vaccine as a child, or if you're not sure if you were vaccinated. They can tell you if you need a catch-up vaccine.
### How is the spread of polio prevented?
If you travel to a country with polio:
- make sure you are vaccinated against polio — ask your doctor if you're not sure
- practise good hygiene
- only drink clean water
- only eat food that has been prepared properly, using clean water

## Complications of polio

Polio may have lasting effects and can be fatal.
Some people who have had polio may develop post-polio syndrome. This is when muscle weakness returns many years after the original infection.
