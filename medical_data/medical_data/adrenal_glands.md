# Adrenal glands

## Key facts

- Your adrenal glands sit on top of your kidneys.
- Your adrenal glands make many hormones, including cortisol, aldosterone and adrenaline.
- Some conditions affecting the adrenal glands include Cushing's syndrome and Addison's disease.
- Adrenal gland conditions are rare.

## What are my adrenal glands?

Your adrenal glands are 2 triangle-shaped endocrine glands that sit on top of your kidneys. They form part of your body's hormonal system .

## What do my adrenal glands do?

Your adrenal glands make hormones that are involved in:
- controlling your blood pressure
- your metabolism
- your body's response to stress

## What hormones do my adrenal glands make?

Your adrenal glands make many hormones, including:
- adrenaline
- cortisol
- aldosterone
Adrenaline is involved in your body's stress (or 'fight or flight') response. It:
- makes your heart beat faster
- sends more blood to your muscles
- causes other changes in your body that prepare it for fight or flight
Cortisol does many things, including:
- influencing your metabolism (how your body uses energy)
- changing your blood sugar levels
- lowering inflammation
- helping to control your blood pressure and heart rate
Aldosterone also plays a part in controlling blood pressure and helps keep your salt and water levels balanced.
The adrenal glands also make a hormone called dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA) and small amounts of testosterone.

## What medical conditions are related to my adrenal glands?

Adrenal gland disorders are rare.
Some conditions that can affect your adrenal glands are:
- Cushing's syndrome
- Addison's disease
- congenital adrenal hyperplasia
- Conn syndrome
Adrenal fatigue is not currently recognised as a medical condition.
### Cushing's syndrome
Cushing's syndrome is when your body has too much cortisol. This causes a range of symptoms, such as:
- weight gain
- a puffy or rounded face
- thin skin
- bruising
- high blood sugar levels or diabetes
Cushing's syndrome is usually caused by taking corticosteroid medicine for a long time. It can sometimes be caused by a tumour in your adrenal gland.
### Addison's disease
Addison's disease is where your adrenal glands don't make enough cortisol and aldosterone. It's also called primary adrenal insufficiency. Addison's disease is a rare condition.
Symptoms of Addison's disease are:
- poor appetite
- salt cravings
- muscle pain or weakness
- feeling lightheaded
You may also have the following signs:
- darkening of your skin
- weight loss
- tiredness
- nausea and vomiting
- diarrhoea
- irregular menstruation (periods)
### Congenital adrenal hyperplasia
Congenital adrenal hyperplasia is an inherited condition. It's usually found in babies and children.
### Conn syndrome
Conn syndrome is the most common cause of ' primary aldosteronism ' — where there is too much of the hormone aldosterone. This is caused by a benign (non-cancerous) tumour in one of your adrenal glands.
The main treatment for Conn syndrome is surgery to remove your affected adrenal gland.
